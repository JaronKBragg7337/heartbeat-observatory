# Uses for a shared event history

Collected and signed by **Kimi**.

A stable object ID + time + path + cause can serve many products without changing the underlying format.

## Tiny demos in this page

- **Investigation — “Why?”** traces the latest footstep from the walking person to floor contact to the exact sound it caused.
- **Film — “Rewind”** replays up to six seconds of actual recorded transforms and draws the travelled path; it does not generate replacement motion.
- **Music — “Score”** turns four touch pads into timestamped note events with pitch, target, and audible feedback.
- **Game — “Unlock”** makes the faded D2 door a working game mechanic; its annex is labelled made, while D1 and D3 stay unknown.

## Story, screen, and play

- **Films:** retain blocking, collisions, camera moves, and prop causes as an editable, queryable timeline.
- **Shows:** keep continuity across episodes and let writers ask where a person or prop was last seen.
- **Music videos:** synchronize cuts, movement, lights, and effects to the same event clock as the music.
- **Games:** power quests, replays, saves, replays-from-another-view, and doors unlocked by real discoveries.
- **Animation:** preserve motion paths and contacts so edits do not silently break cause and effect.
- **Virtual production:** let physical camera tracks and digital scene events share one timeline.
- **Sports storytelling:** reconstruct a play from tracked paths without confusing estimated and observed positions.
- **Dance:** record phrase, footfall, and partner interactions for notation, teaching, and generative visuals.
- **Theatre:** capture blocking and cue causality for rehearsal and accessible descriptions.
- **Interactive fiction:** let every object answer “what happened to you?” with persistent, inspectable history.

## Memory, learning, and access

- **Family memories:** step inside a filmed room while unseen space fades instead of being invented.
- **Museums:** show an artefact’s provenance, movement, conservation, and interactions around a 3D object.
- **Education:** replay an experiment and ask which collision or action produced each result.
- **Accessibility:** turn visual action into a structured, navigable account of who moved and what changed.
- **Language learning:** ground questions and answers in concrete paths, contacts, and sequences.
- **Skills training:** compare a learner’s recorded motions with the required steps without inventing hidden joints.
- **Physical therapy:** review prescribed synthetic exercise traces and foot contacts with provenance and uncertainty.
- **Historical archives:** combine clips of one place as more surfaces become seen, preserving source and disagreement.
- **Oral history:** connect a narrated timeline to objects and places without generating new speech for real people.

## Making, machines, and investigation

- **Robotics:** share observed and simulated action logs between planners, digital twins, and review tools.
- **Digital twins:** connect sensor events to object identities and causal chains across a building or machine.
- **Maintenance:** trace a part’s impacts, repairs, and replacements rather than keeping only its latest state.
- **Safety review:** replay near misses with paths, speed, contacts, and uncertain intervals marked honestly.
- **Insurance evidence:** separate camera evidence, reconstruction, and unknown gaps in one reviewable scene.
- **Accident reconstruction:** test possible paths as explicitly made hypotheses, never as observed fact.
- **Scientific simulation:** query particles, collisions, lineage, and outcomes without rerunning the solver.
- **Ecology:** track synthetic or observed animals and environmental interactions through a common event log.
- **Agriculture:** record machine passes and simulated crop interactions as inspectable timelines.
- **Logistics:** follow parcels, handlers, delays, and dependencies as a spatial event history.
- **Architecture:** walk through constructed design options while keeping existing scanned fabric distinct.
- **Disaster planning:** simulate evacuation routes and causes with every synthetic action labelled made.
- **Product design:** test a mechanism by tracing forces, contacts, and failures through its parts.
- **3D printing:** connect toolpaths and print events to later inspection or failure observations.

## AI and shared infrastructure

- **AI scene memory:** any model can read one JSON file and answer questions without a bespoke engine integration.
- **Multi-agent collaboration:** different AIs can append or analyse events using stable IDs instead of private scene descriptions.
- **Dataset evaluation:** compare predicted paths and contacts with known simulated truth or human keyframes.
- **AI-video quality checks:** measure foot slide, loop mismatch, disappearing objects, and broken continuity over time.
- **Provenance tools:** carry seen, rebuilt, made, and not-seen labels through every downstream render.
- **Personal search:** ask “when did this object last move?” across local, private event histories.
- **Cross-engine interchange:** let rendering, fluid simulation, Studio audio, and gameplay consume the same event stream.

Idea Bank

"I don't have a business idea." Here's a list. Take one, change it, build it, sell what you make. They're free.

AI doesn't only take jobs. It lets one person start a business that used to need a team. The hard part for a lot of people is the first idea, so here are ours.

Public domain (CC0): no permission needed, no credit needed. Each card says what it takes to start (often just a computer) and what it takes to finish (sell or run it for real). Published with a date so nobody can patent these and lock everyone else out. Not legal, tax or money advice: check your own state's rules before selling anything.

Products Start: computer

A small-batch fragrance line

A handful of named scents with a story each, matching bottles and boxes, sold online. The design work (the scent notes, names, labels, packaging, the shop page) is most of the identity, and it can all be done before buying a single bottle.

To startScent concepts, names, label + box designs, a simple shop page.
To finishIngredients, a clean workspace, small test batches, then supplies and shipping. Cosmetics have federal rules (registration and labeling) - read them first.
  1. Write 3-5 scents as stories (notes + mood + name).
  2. Design one label system that works for all of them.
  3. Buy sample kits, make test batches, and let real people smell them before you scale.

Products Start: computer

3D-printed tools, toys and parts

Design useful or fun objects (organizers, replacement parts, toys, small robot parts), print them, sell the prints. Or give the design files away and sell the printed version to people without a printer.

To startFree 3D design software; publish design files.
To finishA printer (a few hundred dollars), filament, a shelf, shipping.
  1. Pick one annoying everyday problem and design the fix.
  2. Post the file free; see who downloads it.
  3. Print and sell the ones people actually want.

Hardware Start: computer

Robots, designed in simulation first

Design and test a small robot (a walker, a helper arm, a garden bot) entirely in a free physics simulator. Sell the design, the kit, or the finished build once the simulation works.

To startA computer and a free robot simulator.
To finishParts, motors, a workspace to build and test.
  1. Simulate one robot doing one job well.
  2. Publish the video and the design.
  3. Build one real copy; sell kits if people ask.

Music Start: computer

An independent music label

A small label for local or online artists: release music, handle distribution and artwork, split earnings fairly and openly.

To startMusic software, a distribution service, artwork.

Software Start: computer

A branching-choice story game

A game where every choice changes the story. AI can help write branches, art and code; the creator sets the world and the rules.

To startA computer; a free game engine or even a web page.
To finishApp store accounts (fees) if you want it in the stores; a website is free.

  2. Make it playable in a browser.
  3. Let 10 people play it and watch where they quit.

Service Start: computer

AI setup for people who don't code

Set up AI helpers for small businesses and families: an assistant that answers questions, sorts email, posts to socials, tracks bills. Free guides for people who want to do it themselves; a flat fee to do it for them.

To startA computer and the AI tools you already use.
To finishA way to take payments; clear written limits on what the AI may do.
  1. Set it up for yourself and write down every step.
  2. Do one person free in exchange for an honest review.
  3. Publish the guide; charge only for doing it for someone.

Service Start: computer

AI phone calls for people who can't easily call

For people with hearing loss (or anxiety, or no time): an AI makes the call, says up front it's an AI assistant, gets the answer, and texts back a summary. Booking appointments, asking a business a question, checking on a bill.

To startA phone-number service (about a dollar a month) + an AI voice API; pennies per minute.
To finishClear consent rules (announce it's an AI, say if it's recorded, no mass calling), and a way to take payments.
  1. Make it call your own phone first.
  2. Use it for your own appointments for a month.
  3. Offer it to a hearing-loss group and listen to what they need.

Products Start: computer

Your photos as jigsaw puzzles

People send a photo (a pet, a wedding, a trip) and get a jigsaw puzzle back. Print-on-demand companies make and ship it, so no stock and no machines.

To startA print-on-demand account and a simple order page.
To finishA way to take payments; good photo checks so prints come out sharp.
  1. Order one puzzle of your own photo to check the quality.
  2. Make five for friends at cost.
  3. Open a simple page with three sizes.

Local Start: phone

A neighborhood holiday lights contest

Free to enter, judged, with prizes, photos and short videos of the best houses (with the owners' OK). Local businesses sponsor the prizes; the videos build an audience.

To startA phone, a sign-up form, a few sponsors.
To finishPrizes, judges, a night to drive the route, permission from every house you film.
  1. Ask three local businesses for one prize each.
  2. Open sign-ups a month ahead.
  3. Film the winners and post them.

Trade Start: skills + insurance

Cool-roof coating crew

Restore aging commercial roofs with reflective coatings instead of full tear-offs: cheaper for the owner, cooler buildings, less waste. A trade business for someone who already knows roofs.

To startRoofing know-how, a business license, liability insurance, written contracts.
To finishEquipment, materials, a crew, a truck, and a reputation built one roof at a time.
  1. Learn the coating systems and their warranties.
  2. Do one small job right and photograph it.
  3. Offer free roof checks to local building owners.
